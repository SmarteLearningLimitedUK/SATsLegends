# Maths curriculum and answer audit

Reviewed 20 October 2026. Target: England, end-of-KS2 mathematics SATs. **Verdict: answer and marking defects have been corrected, but complete curriculum and exam-format coverage is not yet demonstrated.** Do not describe the current game as covering every SATs maths requirement.

The audit maps the 37 current game types, the shared banks and the three mixed-paper generators. It distinguishes a mathematically correct answer from a well-designed assessment task, and distinguishes a topic label from actual coverage of an objective.

## Content-depth expansion

The current [content-depth implementation review](../qa-artifacts/content-depth/index.html) supersedes the earlier follow-through counts. It records **53 additional task families (192 total)**, **99 representative / 96 partial / 0 unmatched objectives**, and 30,600 independently checked answer fields. All 53 new families are sampled by Core papers and available in the related games’ guided Help practice.

New breadth includes whole-number ordering/rounding and sequences; negative midpoints; full factor sets and mental strategies; ordering fractions above one; decimal place value, rounding and powers of ten; independently chosen multi-step operations; ruler/cylinder/protractor readings; mixed-unit and imperial comparisons; overnight time; area/perimeter relationships; selecting formulae; varied digital rectangle/angle construction; symmetry axes; circle vocabulary and cube nets; complete line/pie construction; scaled pictograms and table completion; sequence generation and exhaustive possibilities; and a reasoned mean task including zero.

Core construction diagrams and their controls occupy separate model/answer regions on phones. Partial plot marks, unique unordered sets, bounded estimates and either rectangle orientation are checked explicitly. The Core checkpoint bank version was advanced to prevent old in-progress answers reopening against a different question selection.

The remaining partial entries are retained with current notes. Physical instrument handling, unrestricted drawing, fully assessed written methods/prose explanations, pupil calibration and broader representation/context coverage are not certified. Digital graphs use supplied axes/scales. “Representative” remains evidence of a matched task, not a claim of exhaustive mastery.

## Earlier recommended changes implemented

The follow-through evidence and all 185 fresh screenshots are in [the implementation review](../qa-artifacts/recommended-changes/index.html). There are now 139 mission families, including 26 new task families. The eight previously unmatched objectives have explicit tasks: 3N2b, 4N2b, 4N3b, 5C1, 5C2, 3M4b, 3M9d and 4G2c. Further additions cover decimal rounding and ordering, scale reading, regular-polygon and isosceles angles, improper/mixed fractions, fractional remainders, circle diameter and multi-step reasoning.

Core answers can be entered independently using a keyboard or landscape-phone answer pad. Both reasoning papers include a three-mark problem; distinct valid algebra pairs are accepted. Supported arithmetic calculations on the saved working board can earn partial method credit. This is bounded automatic marking, not an official examiner or handwritten/prose working assessment. Guided revision keeps answer tiles where appropriate.

The 1,000-seed profile finds 13 unchanged families out of 139, compared with 59 out of 113 in the original review. Both reasoning tails include the highest internal difficulty band. These are structural improvements; pupil trials are still required to calibrate the difficulty curve.

Graph Grabber now generates varied boat-delivery bar charts. Multiplication Mine stages numerical demands more gradually. Mean Machine includes zero values and fractional means at later tiers; broader explanation practice remains limited. At that earlier snapshot, the objective matrix retained 124 partial entries, including practical, breadth and response-format limitations.

## Confirmed corrections

1. **Two wrong shared arithmetic keys:** `84 + 67` selected 141 instead of **151**; `47 × 6` selected 262 instead of **282**. Both keys are fixed. The new independent oracle reproduces those two failures against the captured pre-fix inventory, while the current sample passes.
2. **Curriculum metadata:** replaced fabricated year/objective combinations and broad parent labels with actual objective references. Examples include decimal powers of ten, percentage calculations, fraction operations, formal multiplication/division, symmetry, geometry construction and measurement. Timetable reading is 5S1; line-graph comparison is 5S2. KS1-only foundation labels no longer pretend to be year-specific KS2 objectives.
3. **Algebra task:** a numeric missing-box calculation previously labelled 6A1 did not ask pupils to express a relationship algebraically. It now asks which equation represents the stated relationship using n.
4. **Simplest form:** equivalent but unsimplified fractions such as 6/8 no longer receive the mark when 3/4 in simplest form is requested. Decimal notation is rejected where a fraction is explicitly requested. Valid simplified mixed-number answers remain accepted.
5. **Arithmetic final-answer credit:** a correct final answer now receives both marks in the four two-mark written-arithmetic practice questions, even without the scaffolded intermediate response. A valid intermediate practice step can still earn one mark. This correction does not turn the scaffold into a full examiner-style assessment of written working.
6. **Rectangle construction:** the interactive rectangle could be described as having two right angles. It now always has four; length and width remain editable and determine correctness.
7. **Net recognition and wording:** a task that supplied “this folds into a cube” and only asked for faces/vertices did not test net recognition. It now asks which solid the pictured net makes, followed by its vertices. Distractors avoid the inclusive cube/cuboid ambiguity. A table task no longer calls ordinary numerical rows “tally evidence”.
8. **Coverage assurance:** replaced the old “all 56 sub-strands” success message with a limited topic-family check and an explicit objective-level coverage gate. The earlier route overview also incorrectly described Rounding Rocket as decimal rounding and Area Architect as triangle/parallelogram formula practice; those descriptions are corrected.

Detailed partial-coverage notes matter as much as this short list.

## Comparison with official papers

Used the current framework, statutory curriculum and all three published 2026 papers plus their mark schemes. The framework states that annual papers sample content over time: one year's paper is not a complete syllabus checklist.

| Official example | What it exposes in the current game |
| --- | --- |
| 2026 Paper 2 Q20: populate possible coin combinations | The game has constrained-pair questions, but general open valid-answer sets and table completion need stronger support. |
| 2026 Paper 2 Q22: missing values/midpoints across zero | Negative-number practice exists, but contextual intervals and midpoint reasoning need wider variety. |
| 2026 Paper 2 Q23: combine pie-chart information to recover a total | Current simple pie/percentage items provide a start; mixed representations and unknown-whole reasoning need more depth. |
| 2026 Paper 3 Q17: select all correct equivalence statements | A single “choose one” equivalence task is not the same assessment demand. |
| 2026 Paper 3 Q19: give different valid pairs of unknowns | Current fixed answer sets do not generally accept the range of independently valid responses. |
| 2026 Paper 3 Q20: infer angles from an isosceles diagram | Existing direct angle-total calculations are narrower than this inference task. |
| 2026 Paper 3 Q21: shaded composite area with three marks for answer/method | The game has component area skills, but no equivalent three-mark unscaffolded item/marking structure. |

These are comparison points, not copied additions to the game. Labels, units, diagrams and complete givens must all agree. Shape names also need care with inclusive definitions: a cube is a special cuboid and a square is a special rectangle; multiple-choice distractors must not create a second valid answer.

## Verification and reproducibility

- `npx tsx scripts/export-maths-audit.ts`: captures 36 arithmetic and 77 reasoning families across 125 seeds, 70 shared families and 30 measurement/time/data records, plus paper selections.
- `python scripts/audit-maths-answers.py`: independently recomputes 30,600 answer fields with exact fractions and separate mathematical rules. Its pre-fix comparison catches both wrong shared keys.
- `node scripts/verify-maths-local-banks.mjs`: checks all 798 canonical Ratio Racer records; 1,000 sharing rounds; 1,000 potion ratios; 1,000 percentage rounds; 1,000 division problems; 1,000 perimeters; 40 floor models; four polyhedral property sets.
- Existing checks rerun: 14,400 number-line questions; 2,400 generated angle questions; 750 operation-order rounds; 10,000 transformations; 1,386 column products; 6,320 line/statistics/mean/fraction/formula cases; 600 mock-paper variants and seeded disjoint-paper/choice/normalisation checks.
- New regression checks cover simplest-form/fraction notation, equivalent mixed numbers, arithmetic final-answer credit, genuine equation representation, valid objective identifiers and rectangle resizing/marking.
- Live browser checks cover all three paper flows at desktop and landscape phone sizes: answer, save, revisit, finish, review and local result persistence. Remote account-save indicators were excluded from this content review.
- `npm run lint` (TypeScript) and `npm run build` passed. The normal existing large-chunk advisory is separate from maths correctness.
- `node scripts/audit-maths-coverage.mjs --strict` deliberately exits unsuccessfully while known partial/unmatched objectives remain. The default command writes the review matrix without disguising the coverage verdict as a pass.

The automated sample is substantial but does not prove every random run of every local generator, the educational validity of every distractor, classroom mastery, practical ruler/protractor skill or official examiner accreditation. The matrix keeps those limits explicit. A subject-specialist review of depth, wording and diagram inference remains part of closing the listed gaps.

## Assumptions and scope

England KS2 SATs, normally the end of Year 6. Existing game mechanics, art and progression were retained. Original questions are assessed against the official scope; no annual paper is treated as a complete curriculum or copied into the game. Only current repository material, the present request and the explicitly researched official sources were used. **No external legacy assumptions were used.**
