## Findings that determine the remediation order

1. **P1 — primary educational instruments are too small in several short-landscape compositions.** Clock face, angle diagram, perimeter edge labels and match tiles lose priority to reserved scenery and character space. Correct the allocation of stage space before changing illustration quality. The large world artwork itself is not the defect.
2. **P1 — Rounding Rocket's opening interaction lacks visual presence.** The rocket is a faint, desaturated silhouette in an otherwise spacious portrait scene. `src/games/rounding-rocket.css` explicitly sets `.rr-blueprint { opacity: .2; filter: saturate(0); }`. This conveys an unbuilt state, but the main game object is hard to distinguish from the coast. Preserve the unbuilt/installed distinction with a readable construction silhouette and visibly emphasized next part; do not simply make every part appear installed. Use the central sky for readable part progression instead of further widening the answer panel.
3. **P1 — Zombies separates the actors but sacrifices the scene.** Current capture clearly shows grounded actors and an oval footing; do not report the earlier floating-hero defect as still reproduced. However `src/games/maths-vs-zombies.css` applies brightness `.7`, saturation `.55` and blur `1.5px` to the entire outer backdrop, plus `.62/.48` on its fallback. The screenshot visibly loses the vibrant established scenery. Replace the broad treatment with a restrained region behind the combat lane, a carefully cropped quieter region and grounded contact shadow. Keep actors readable without thick outlines or ubiquitous glow. This is a specific scene defect, not a reason to globally filter other games.
4. **P2 — repeated pose silhouettes remain visible across the catalogue.** `VisionCompanion.tsx` selects only four helper art poses (`blueprint`, `guide`, `navigate`, `carry`), and `originalHeroActivities.ts` samples the same four asset cells. Blueprint is reused for Area, Multiplication, Formula, Scale and Paper 3; map/navigation repeats elsewhere. Current images show some successful left/right staging variation, so a blanket 'every scene uses the same pose' is inaccurate. Use existing poses when meaningful; build a pose/contact-sheet plan before commissioning additional art. Change art only where the scene role cannot be conveyed by existing assets.
5. **P2 — decorative text and duplicated panels compete with the task.** Examples: Match Mastery has a second timer/XP row, Transformation a second small status strip, and large blank panel regions surround some controls. Shared Tips is useful, but mathematical constraints, current state, axes and units must stay on screen. A panel being inside the viewport is not evidence that its numbers are legible.

## Phase 3B — character visibility, focal hierarchy and background separation

Added from the user's follow-up brief on22 October2026. This strengthens the existing scene ledger; it does not reopen completed investigation or authorise a blanket visual treatment. Implementation remains roadmap B4 after usable scene bounds are stable.

**The character and interaction must be unmistakable.** When a character is decorative, the mathematical task takes priority. Never enlarge a guide at the expense of diagram labels, questions or answers. A character does not need to occupy every scene; Prime Pop's moving numbers can be its primary subject.

For every game in the scene table, create one evidence record per supported composition. Include game/level/question/state, viewport, build, visible background source, intended primary subject, observed first focal point, specific competing region, cause category, proposed least-destructive change, before/after paths, regression result and remaining uncertainty. Cause categories: artwork, crop/composition, scale/layout, local colour/luminance, foreground occlusion, layering and motion. Record `not yet verified` rather than inventing a first-time-player result.

### Scene-local correction sequence

1. Confirm which background layer is actually visible. Inspect computed styles and rendered source; do not tune an obscured fallback.
2. Give the interaction adequate space and a clean silhouette. Reposition/crop scenery or decorative actors before adding effects. Check hero gaze/gesture supports the active target.
3. Where colour or detail still competes, adjust only the local region behind the subject: soften competing highlights/detail, shift environmental colour balance or add restrained atmospheric depth. Keep surrounding rich scenery intact. A warm hero may benefit from a cooler quiet region; a blue object against water may need a credible contrasting surface.
4. Ground actors and objects on their actual scene support using alpha-aware feet/contact bounds. Check idle, action and feedback frames. Use restrained contact shadow or environmental lighting only where needed; no universal halo, outline or podium.
5. Preserve established character designs. New art is a last resort justified by a demonstrated asset/composition limitation, not the default fix.

### State language and focal tests

Interactive, selected, disabled, correct and incorrect states must remain distinct without colour alone: use existing pressed/selected treatment, check/error symbols, labels or shape/border changes as appropriate. Draggable sources and drop destinations need a clear affordance and visible selected/accepted state; decorative scenery must not impersonate a target. Do not add every cue to every object.

Review full-size phone captures first, then reduced-size and grayscale comparisons as diagnostic aids. Assess the subject against its immediate backdrop, not average whole-image contrast. Record what draws the reviewer's eye first and why. This is an art-direction judgement, not a measured user-study outcome. Automated contrast/bounds checks support human review and cannot certify visual hierarchy.

Use same-state before/after pairs. Check idle, selected/dragging, correct/incorrect feedback and peak animation; compare portrait/landscape only where each is supported. For a gated alternative capture the turn/Start state rather than pretend an unsupported composition passes. Larger-window exploration remains separate from native iPhone certification.

A Phase3B scene is complete only when the primary subject is immediately identifiable at normal mobile size, interaction is obvious, essential maths remains readable, grounded frames have credible support, state cues do not rely solely on colour, background richness is preserved, and before/after renders plus relevant interaction tests show no regression. Otherwise retain the scene as open with its exact failed criterion.

## Acceptance checklist

1. Native-size 844 × 390, 667 × 375 and 390 × 844 review as applicable; include smallest supported native safe viewport separately. iPad full/windowed evidence is still required.
2. Read question, diagram labels, units and answers at normal viewing scale without enlarging screenshot. Essential labels must not be tiny merely because a bounds test passes.
3. No actor or its action/reward frame enters mathematical labels, answer hit areas or dock. Intentional overlap of scenery is allowed and often desirable.
4. Actor feet/contact stay on a credible support in every grounded frame; flying objects have clear intentional motion.
5. A first-time viewer identifies the active task before decorative background highlights. Grayscale/reduced-size comparison is useful as supplementary evidence, not a mechanical pass/fail for rich art.
6. Verify opening, longest question, selected/dragging, correct, incorrect, completion and pause/Tips states. Five difficulty tiers and three paper interaction families must not be represented by one opening screenshot.
7. Retain rich colour outside a localized focal treatment. No catalogue-wide dark/blur filter, thick outline or glow retrofit.
8. Audit is not deployment approval. No TestFlight upload until the user's release hold is explicitly lifted.
